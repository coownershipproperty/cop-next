# COP Translation Glossary

Locked terminology for every locale COP publishes in — currently English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Brazilian Portuguese, Swedish, Danish and Norwegian. Covers property descriptions, blog posts, town guides and marketing copy. **Every translation must conform to this glossary** — no synonyms, no drift.

> **The single most important rule in this file:** the words for our product are *false friends across languages*. Italian `multiproprietà` means **timeshare**. Brazilian Portuguese `multipropriedade` means **our exact product**, as regulated by Lei nº 13.777/2018. Norwegian `tidspart` is the statutory name for timeshare. Never translate product terminology from one locale into another — always translate from the English source, then check it against that locale's section below.

The glossary captures three things per locale: the **primary term** (always used at first mention in a piece of content), **acceptable secondary terms** (variations after context is established), and **forbidden terms** that have negative SEO or legal connotations and must never appear.

## French (`fr`)

### Why this matters

`copropriété` is legally pre-loaded in French (Loi du 10 juillet 1965 — apartment building common areas). Used alone, it confuses French buyers. Prello solved this by always pairing it with `résidence secondaire` and using `à plusieurs` as the action verb. Lazazu solves it by using the verb form `co-posséder`.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention of the product:
- `copropriété de résidence secondaire`

**Secondary** — variations once the context is established within a piece of content:
- `copropriété` (only after the full compound has appeared at least once)
- `copropriétaire` (the person)
- `quote-part de résidence secondaire`
- `co-ownership` (used in French press; acceptable as anglicism)

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `co-posséder une résidence secondaire`
- `acheter une résidence secondaire à plusieurs`
- `acquérir une quote-part`

**Legal vehicle**:
- `LLC` (Limited Liability Company) — the standard COP-wide structure across France, Spain, Italy, Portugal and the US. Use `LLC` or `société à responsabilité limitée (LLC)` on first mention in French copy, then `LLC` thereafter.
- `membership interest` — the COP-wide term for what each co-owner holds in the LLC
- `operating agreement` — the LLC's governing document (replaces French `statuts` in COP terminology)
- `acte authentique chez le notaire`
- `cadastre` (land registry)

**Consumer category anchor** (always used near the product term):
- `résidence secondaire`
- `maison de vacances` (more emotional/aspirational variant — fine in lifestyle copy)

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `multipropriété` | Timeshare in French — heavy negative connotation, regulated category. Only use in comparison content where we explicitly contrast it with copropriété. |
| `temps partagé` | Legal name for timeshare (Code de la consommation) — using it incorrectly could trigger consumer protection law. |
| `immobilier fractionné` | Strongly associated with crowdfunded investment platforms (Tantiem, Bricks, Baltis). Wrong audience — they're yield investors, not lifestyle buyers. |
| `propriété partagée` (alone) | Descriptive but vague; doesn't rank well. Use `copropriété de résidence secondaire` instead. |

### Place names (French exonyms)

- Spain → Espagne · Mallorca → Majorque · Menorca → Minorque · Ibiza → Ibiza
- Italy → Italie · Sardinia → Sardaigne · Liguria → Ligurie · Lake Como → Lac de Côme · Lake Garda → Lac de Garde
- USA → États-Unis · California → Californie · Florida → Floride
- England → Angleterre · London → Londres
- Austria → Autriche · Tyrol → Tyrol · Salzburg → Salzbourg
- Germany → Allemagne · Bavaria → Bavière · Berlin → Berlin
- Mexico → Mexique · Sweden → Suède · Croatia → Croatie · Portugal → Portugal · Algarve → Algarve
- Côte d'Azur stays Côte d'Azur · Paris stays Paris

## German (`de`)

### Why this matters

German has a clean legal term for what we sell — `Miteigentum nach Bruchteilen` (fractional co-ownership under §§ 1008 ff. BGB) — and a badly poisoned one, `Teilzeitwohnrecht` / `Timesharing`, regulated under §§ 481 ff. BGB. The two are legally unrelated: Miteigentum is registered ownership in the Grundbuch; Teilzeitwohnrecht is a contractual right of use. German buyers know the difference and care about it, so the Grundbuch anchor does real conversion work, not just SEO.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `Miteigentum an einer Ferienimmobilie`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `Miteigentum` · `Bruchteilseigentum` · `Miteigentumsanteil` (the share) · `Miteigentümer` (the person)
- `anteiliges Eigentum`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `eine Ferienimmobilie gemeinsam kaufen`
- `einen Miteigentumsanteil erwerben`

**Trust anchor** (use early, it is the single strongest signal in German):
- `im Grundbuch eingetragen`

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `Ferienimmobilie` · `Ferienhaus` · `Zweitwohnsitz`

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `Teilzeitwohnrecht` | The statutory term for timeshare (§ 481 BGB). Legally a *use right*, not ownership — using it would misdescribe the product. Comparison content only. |
| `Timesharing` / `Timeshare` | Same category, decades of German press coverage of Spanish-coast fraud. Comparison content only. |
| `Ferienwohnrecht` | Reads as a use right rather than title. Vague and weak in search. |
| `Immobilien-Crowdinvesting` / `tokenisierte Immobilie` | Yield-investor category (Exporo etc.). Wrong audience entirely. |

## Italian (`it`)

### Why this matters

**This is the highest-stakes terminology decision of the whole language build.** In Italian, `multiproprietà` means **timeshare** — it is the standard consumer term for exactly the product COP spends its time distinguishing itself from, and it carries the same fraud associations Spanish `multipropiedad` does. It is also, unfortunately, the highest-volume Italian search term in this space. Ranking for it is worth something; being *described* by it is not.

Note the inversion against Brazilian Portuguese below, where the near-identical word `multipropriedade` is the correct regulated legal regime. Same word, opposite meanings, one country apart. Translators must not carry the term across.

The correct Italian frame is `comproprietà` (co-ownership generally, Art. 1100 ff. Codice Civile) paired with `proprietà frazionata` (fractional ownership), anchored to `casa vacanze` so the buyer knows we mean a holiday home, not a disputed inherited apartment — which is what `comproprietà` alone connotes in Italy, since most Italian co-ownership is involuntary and inherited.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `comproprietà di una casa vacanze`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `comproprietà` · `proprietà frazionata` · `quota di proprietà` (the share) · `comproprietario` (the person)
- `acquisto frazionato`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `comprare una casa vacanze in comproprietà`
- `acquistare una quota di una casa vacanze`

**Trust anchor**:
- `atto notarile` · `iscritto al Catasto` · `proprietà reale, non un diritto d'uso`

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `casa vacanze` · `seconda casa`

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `multiproprietà` | **Timeshare in Italian.** Regulated under the Codice del Consumo as a *diritto di godimento a tempo parziale*. Never describes our product. Comparison content only, and always explicitly contrasted. |
| `diritto di godimento a tempo parziale` | The statutory name for the above. Comparison content only. |
| `timeshare` | English loan used in Italian press for the same category. |
| `investimento immobiliare frazionato` | Crowdfunding/yield category (Walliance, Concrete). Wrong audience. |
| `nuda proprietà` | Means bare ownership (owner minus usufruct) — a completely different Italian product, heavily searched, and confusing it with ours would be a serious mis-sell. |

### Place names (Italian exonyms)

- Spain → Spagna · Mallorca → Maiorca · Menorca → Minorca · Ibiza → Ibiza

## Dutch (`nl`)

### Why this matters

Dutch is the easiest of the new locales: `timeshare` stayed an English loanword in Dutch, so there is no native term to collide with and no word we have to defend. The risk here is different — `mede-eigendom` is the correct legal phrase but is heavily used in Belgian apartment-building contexts (Belgian *mede-eigendom* law governs shared parts of a building, much like French copropriété). Pairing it with `vakantiewoning` disambiguates immediately.

The Dutch second-home buyer is unusually well-informed and price-literate, and Dutch marketing copy that oversells reads as untrustworthy. Keep Dutch copy flatter and more factual than the Southern-European locales — that is a register choice, not a tone exception.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `mede-eigendom van een vakantiewoning`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `mede-eigendom` · `gedeeld eigendom` · `fractioneel eigendom` · `aandeel` (the share) · `mede-eigenaar` (the person)
- `samen een vakantiehuis kopen`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `samen een vakantiewoning kopen`
- `een aandeel in een vakantiewoning kopen`

**Trust anchor**:
- `notarieel vastgelegd` · `op uw naam ingeschreven` · `echt eigendom, geen gebruiksrecht`

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `vakantiewoning` · `vakantiehuis` · `tweede woning`

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `timeshare` | Same category and connotation as everywhere else. Comparison content only. |
| `deeltijdwoning` / `deeltijd vakantiewoning` | Reads as timeshare's Dutch descriptive form. Avoid. |
| `vastgoedfonds` / `vastgoed-crowdfunding` | Yield-investor category. Wrong audience. |
| `erfpacht` | Leasehold — a live and sensitive topic in the Netherlands. Never let it appear near our product description. |

## Portuguese — Brazil (`pt`)

### Why this matters

**Read this before touching a word of Portuguese.** In Brazil, `multipropriedade` is the *correct* term. It is the name of the regulated legal regime created by **Lei nº 13.777/2018**, which amended the Código Civil to establish multipropriedade imobiliária as a real property right (*direito real*), registered on the property's matrícula at the Cartório de Registro de Imóveis. It is exactly, precisely what COP sells — deeded fractional ownership with a fixed usage calendar. Brazilians search for it by that name, lawyers write about it by that name, and avoiding it would cost us the entire market.

This is the direct inverse of Italian, where the cognate `multiproprietà` means timeshare. **Never translate between these two locales.** Translate each from the English source.

Second decision: this locale targets **Brazil**, per the brief. That drives vocabulary (`imóvel` over `imobiliário` as the noun, `cartório`, `matrícula`, `você` not `tu`, `time-sharing` written the Brazilian way) and hreflang (`pt-BR`, with a plain `pt` fallback so Portugal still resolves). Portuguese-from-Portugal terminology — `direito real de habitação periódica`, `propriedade fracionada` — is *not* used here; if we ever build a `pt-PT` locale it needs its own research pass, because in Portugal the periodic-habitation right is the timeshare category and would be actively wrong.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `multipropriedade` (always introduced with the legal anchor on first use in a page: *regulamentada pela Lei nº 13.777/2018*)

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `fração imobiliária` · `cota` (the share) · `copropriedade` · `coproprietário` (the person)
- `casa de férias compartilhada`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `comprar uma cota de uma casa de férias`
- `ser dono de uma fração de um imóvel de férias`

**Trust anchor** (unusually powerful in Brazil — use it early):
- `registrada na matrícula do imóvel` · `Cartório de Registro de Imóveis` · `direito real de propriedade` · `Lei nº 13.777/2018`

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `casa de férias` · `imóvel de férias` · `segunda casa`

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `time-sharing` / `timeshare` | The pre-2018 unregulated contractual product Brazilians associate with hotel-lobby hard-sells. Our whole argument is that Lei 13.777 replaced it. Comparison content only. |
| `direito real de habitação periódica` | Portuguese (Portugal) timeshare category. Wrong country, wrong product. |
| `propriedade fracionada` | Not wrong, but it is the Portugal-flavoured phrasing and dilutes the Lei 13.777 anchor. Use `fração imobiliária` instead. |
| `investimento fracionado` / `tokenização imobiliária` | Yield/crypto category. Wrong audience, and a regulatory minefield with CVM. |

### Place names (Brazilian Portuguese exonyms)

- Spain → Espanha · Mallorca → Maiorca · Menorca → Menorca · Ibiza → Ibiza
- France → França · French Alps → Alpes Franceses · Côte d'Azur → Costa Azul · Paris → Paris
- Italy → Itália · Sardinia → Sardenha · Lake Como → Lago de Como · Lake Garda → Lago de Garda
- USA → Estados Unidos · California → Califórnia · Florida → Flórida
- England → Inglaterra · London → Londres
- Austria → Áustria · Germany → Alemanha · Bavaria → Baviera · Sweden → Suécia

## Swedish (`sv`)

### Why this matters

Sweden already has a strong native culture of shared holiday-home ownership — siblings inheriting a *sommarstuga* together — and a body of law for it, the **samäganderättslagen (1904:48)**. That is an advantage and a trap. The advantage: Swedes understand co-ownership instantly, no education needed. The trap: their mental model is the *inherited family cottage with no agreement and no exit*, which Swedish legal blogs write about mainly as a source of disputes. So Swedish copy must lead with the things that distinguish a managed, structured share from an unmanaged family one: a written agreement, a booking calendar, professional management, and a defined way to sell.

Timeshare in Swedish is `tidsdelat boende` (or the loanword), which is distinct enough from `samägande` that there is little collision risk — but `andelsägande` sits closer to the timeshare marketing language of the 1990s, so it is secondary, not primary.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `samägande av fritidshus`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `samägande` · `delägarskap` · `delägare` (the person) · `andel` (the share) · `andelsägande`
- `äga fritidshus tillsammans`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `köpa ett fritidshus tillsammans`
- `köpa en andel i ett semesterhus`

**Trust anchor**:
- `lagfaren ägare` · `inskrivet i fastighetsregistret` · `riktigt ägande, inte nyttjanderätt`
- `samäganderättsavtal` (the agreement — Swedes will look for this by name)

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `fritidshus` · `semesterhus` · `semesterbostad`

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `tidsdelat boende` / `timeshare` | Swedish timeshare category. Comparison content only. |
| `veckoandel` | Reads directly as a timeshare week. Never use for our share. |
| `fastighetsfond` / `fastighetscrowdfunding` | Yield-investor category. Wrong audience. |
| `bostadsrätt` | Means a Swedish housing-cooperative right — a specific and completely different domestic tenure. Using it would be factually wrong. |

## Danish (`da`)

### Why this matters

Danish is close to Swedish in structure but differs in one commercially important way: Danes overwhelmingly buy their second homes *abroad* — Spain, France, Italy, Portugal — because Danish rules on foreign nationals and on summerhouse ownership push demand outward, and because the Danish *sommerhus* is a domestic category with its own zoning culture. So Danish copy should lean harder into the abroad story (Costa del Sol, Provence, the Alps, Tuscany) than the Swedish or Norwegian versions do.

Timeshare in Danish is just `timeshare`, governed by the Timeshareloven, so there is no native term to defend. `Medejerskab` is clean, commercially used, and reads naturally; `sameje` is the legal term and is best kept for the legal/FAQ register rather than headlines.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `medejerskab af feriebolig`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `medejerskab` · `delt ejerskab` · `medejer` (the person) · `andel` (the share)
- `sameje` (legal register only — FAQs, terms, glossary)
- `at eje et feriehus sammen`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `købe en feriebolig sammen`
- `købe en andel af et feriehus i Spanien`

**Trust anchor**:
- `tinglyst ejerskab` · `registreret i dit navn` · `rigtigt ejerskab, ikke en brugsret`

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `feriebolig` · `feriehus` · `sommerhus` (domestic connotation — prefer `feriebolig` for abroad)

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `timeshare` | Danish timeshare category (Timeshareloven). Comparison content only. |
| `ferieandel` / `ugeandel` | Reads as a timeshare week. Never use for our share. |
| `anpartslejlighed` | A specific, historical Danish tax-driven apartment structure with its own SKAT treatment. Would badly confuse a Danish reader. |
| `ejendomscrowdfunding` | Yield-investor category. Wrong audience. |

## Norwegian (`no`, Bokmål)

### Why this matters

Norwegian has the single cleanest legal fit of any locale in this build. **Sameie** — governed by the **sameigelova (lov om sameige, 1965)** — *is* fractional co-ownership of real property, with each owner holding an *ideell andel* (an ideal, undivided share) registered in **Grunnboken**. It is the exact legal shape of what COP sells, it is a term every Norwegian adult already understands, and hytte-sameie between families is completely normal. We should use it plainly and without hedging.

The word to keep away from is `tidspart`. Norway's timeshare statute is literally the **tidspartloven** (lov om salg av tidsparter i fritidsbolig, 1997), so `tidspart` is the legal name of the category we are not. It is not a connotation problem — it is a definitional one.

Norway is also `&Hamlet`'s home market, which means Norwegian buyers are the most likely of any locale to already know the category. Copy can therefore assume more and explain less, and should compete on inventory breadth and on the abroad story rather than on educating the reader.

### Locked terms

**Primary** — used at first mention:
- `sameie i fritidsbolig`

**Secondary** — once context is established:
- `sameie` · `sameieandel` · `ideell andel` (the share, legal register) · `sameier` / `medeier` (the person)
- `delt eierskap` · `eie hytte sammen`

**Action verbs / phrases**:
- `kjøpe fritidsbolig sammen`
- `kjøpe en andel i en feriebolig`

**Trust anchor**:
- `tinglyst i Grunnboken` · `registrert på ditt navn` · `reelt eierskap, ikke bruksrett`
- `sameieavtale` (the agreement — Norwegians will look for this by name)

**Consumer category anchor**:
- `fritidsbolig` · `feriebolig` · `hytte` (domestic/mountain connotation — use for Alps content, not Mediterranean)

### Forbidden terms — NEVER USE

| Term | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| `tidspart` / `tidsparter` | **The statutory name for timeshare** (tidspartloven). Definitionally wrong for our product, not merely off-tone. Comparison content only. |
| `timeshare` | Same category, used in Norwegian press. Comparison content only. |
| `borettslag` / `andelsleilighet` | Norwegian housing co-operative — a specific and completely different domestic tenure. Factually wrong for us. |
| `eiendomscrowdfunding` | Yield-investor category. Wrong audience. |

### Place names (Nordic exonyms — sv / da / no)

- Spain → Spanien / Spanien / Spania · France → Frankrike / Frankrig / Frankrike
- Italy → Italien / Italien / Italia · Germany → Tyskland (all three) · Austria → Österrike / Østrig / Østerrike
- USA → USA (all three) · England → England (all three) · Mexico → Mexiko / Mexico / Mexico

## Style rules (all locales)

1. **Strip all partner brand mentions** — MYNE, Vivla, Pacaso, And Hamlet, Lazazu, Prello — replace with generic descriptions. COP is an independent aggregator and cannot name partner brands on its own pages.
2. **Preserve markdown bold markers** (`**text**`) exactly. They render as visual emphasis on the property page.
3. **Tone**: upscale, factual, aspirational but grounded. Not AI-translated-sounding.
4. **Number and currency formatting** — driven by `LOCALE_META[locale].numberLocale` in `lib/i18n.js`, but for hand-written copy:

   | Locale | Thousands | Example | Currency placement |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `en` | comma | `€100,000` | before |
   | `es` | period | `€100.000` | before |
   | `fr` | narrow space | `100 000 €` | after (non-breaking space) |
   | `de` | period | `100.000 €` | after (non-breaking space) |
   | `it` | period | `100.000 €` | after |
   | `nl` | period | `€ 100.000` | before, with a space |
   | `pt` | period | `€ 100.000` | before, with a space |
   | `sv` | space | `100 000 €` | after |
   | `da` | period | `100.000 €` | after |
   | `no` | space | `100 000 €` | after |

5. **Length**: match the English source length within ~10%. Don't summarise or expand.
6. **First paragraph** of any page or property description must contain that locale's **primary term** at least once, and the **trust anchor** (registry/notary phrase) should appear within the first two paragraphs — it is the highest-converting element in every locale we have data for.
7. **Tone standard** (applies to every locale, carried over from the town-guide tone pass): sell the place, keep the facts. State trade-offs plainly and once, in the register of a well-informed friend rather than a warning label. Never frame a destination or a season as a problem to be survived.
8. **Never machine-translate between two non-English locales.** Always translate from the English source and verify against this file — see the false-friends warning at the top.

## Maintenance

Update this file when:
- A new keyword research pass identifies a better-ranking term
- Google Search Console data shows we're missing a high-volume term
- A new locale is added — write its section here **before** writing a single word of copy in it

Locales deliberately *not* built, and why:
- **`pt-PT` (Portugal)** — needs its own research pass. In Portugal the periodic-habitation right (`direito real de habitação periódica`) is the timeshare category, so Brazilian copy cannot be reused there without becoming actively wrong.
- **`fr-BE` / `nl-BE` (Belgium)** — Belgian `mede-eigendom` / `copropriété` law differs from the Dutch and French cases; the existing `nl` and `fr` pages serve Belgium acceptably via hreflang fallback.

Verification: run `node scripts/verify-translations.js` after any translation batch — it checks every translated row in Supabase for forbidden terms and prints a report. The forbidden-term lists it enforces are generated from the tables above, so adding a row here is enough to make the checker enforce it.

1. **Property descriptions.** `translatedLocales()` in `lib/i18n.js` requires
   both a title and a description before a locale's property URL is indexable,
   so ~350 property pages per locale are currently `noindex,follow`. They stay
   that way until `description_{loc}` is populated. This is the single biggest
   unlock left: roughly 2,100 pages.
2. **Property titles.** `scripts/translate-property-titles.mjs` composes them
   from the three vocabularies in that file and covers 409 of 443 rows; the
   remaining 34 are bespoke titles it deliberately refuses to guess at. Dry run
   with `node scripts/translate-property-titles.mjs`, apply with
   `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY=… node scripts/translate-property-titles.mjs --write`
   (same convention as `scripts/translate-titles-bulk.js`). Titles alone do not
   lift the noindex — descriptions are the other half — but they do make each
   locale's listings page genuinely native, which is its second-most-visited page.
3. **Town guides.** `content/towns/{slug}.json` holds `en`/`de`/`fr`/`es`
   section arrays. Adding a locale key there and setting `towns` in
   `ROUTE_SLUGS` publishes 20 deep pages for that language.
4. **Destination pages**, then the FAQ, glossary, compare and partner corpora.

### Two rules that must not be relaxed

- **Never machine-translate between two non-English locales.** Translate from
  the English source and check against this file. Italian `multiproprietà`
  means timeshare; Brazilian `multipropriedade` is our exact product.
- **Never publish a locale URL whose body is still English.** A page with
  Norwegian navigation and an English description is thin content, and at the
  scale of a property catalogue it damages the whole domain rather than just
  that page. The `noindex` gate exists to make the safe thing automatic — do
  not route around it to make a launch look bigger.
