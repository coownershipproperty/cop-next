The cooling-off-period refund

The statutory cooling-off period (7-14 days in EU jurisdictions; varies elsewhere) is the buyer's structural protection. During this window:

  • The buyer can withdraw without giving a reason
  • Full deposit refund follows formal withdrawal notice
  • Typical refund timeline: 14-30 days from notice
  • No financial penalty applies
  • The reservation is cancelled and the share returns to market

The cooling-off mechanism is designed to give buyers time for legal and tax review with their own specialists before final commitment. Operators include it because it's required by consumer-protection law in most EU jurisdictions and because credible operators see it as part of treating buyers fairly.

How to use the cooling-off period

Three productive uses during cooling-off. First, engage your own lawyer to review the LLC operating agreement and SPA — see should I use a lawyer? Second, engage your cross-border tax specialist to confirm the home-country position. Three, complete any final due diligence items (property documentation review; existing-owner conversations where applicable).

The cooling-off period isn't passive — it's the buyer's structured opportunity to verify the purchase before commitment.

Withdrawing during cooling-off

Process for formal withdrawal during cooling-off:

  1. Buyer notifies the operator in writing (typically email plus formal letter)
  2. Operator acknowledges receipt and confirms withdrawal
  3. Reservation is cancelled; share returns to market
  4. Operator initiates deposit refund within the timeline specified in the SPA
  5. Refund arrives in buyer's bank account 14-30 days from withdrawal

The mechanics are designed to be straightforward; no negotiation required during cooling-off.

After cooling-off has closed

Refund mechanics for withdrawals after the cooling-off period depend on the SPA's specific terms. Three common patterns.

Partial refund for exceptional circumstances. Some operators offer 50-75% deposit refund if the buyer withdraws for documented exceptional circumstances (significant unplanned change in financial position, family emergency, severe illness). Discretionary, but credible operators handle exceptional situations sympathetically.

Forfeiture for routine withdrawal. If the buyer withdraws after cooling-off for routine reasons (changed their mind; found a different property), the deposit is typically forfeited per SPA terms.

Partial-refund-with-cost-recovery. Some operators offer refund minus documented costs incurred (legal fees, KYC processing, marketing-period opportunity cost). Net refund typically 70-85% of deposit.

What the deposit typically represents

Three things the deposit functionally does. First, demonstrates buyer commitment — separates serious buyers from researchers. Second, holds the share off the market during cooling-off and KYC period. Three, covers operator costs incurred during reservation period (administration, legal documentation preparation, KYC processing).

The deposit size (typically €5,000-€20,000) is calibrated to be meaningful for the buyer without representing dramatic financial commitment if cooling-off withdrawal happens.

What buyers should verify in the SPA

Five things relating to deposit and refund mechanics. First, exact deposit amount and payment timing. Second, cooling-off period length and trigger date. Three, withdrawal notification mechanism. Four, refund timeline post-withdrawal. Five, post-cooling-off withdrawal terms.

What the cooling-off period doesn't cover

Three things that aren't typically covered by cooling-off provisions. Annual fee changes during ownership (governed separately by the operating agreement). Special assessments after purchase (operating-agreement-defined process). Resale process timing (operator's standard mechanism, not affected by cooling-off).

What if there's a dispute about the refund

Three escalation paths if needed. First, formal complaint through the operator's owner-services team. Second, mediation under the SPA's dispute-resolution provisions. Three, statutory consumer-protection authorities in the relevant jurisdiction (most EU countries have specific authorities for property-related consumer-protection issues).

In practice, deposit-refund disputes are extremely rare. Quality operators handle cooling-off withdrawal smoothly as standard practice.
