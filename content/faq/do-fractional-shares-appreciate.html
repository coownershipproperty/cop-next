The short answer: Yes. A fractional share is a deeded ownership interest in a property-holding LLC, so the share's value moves with the underlying real estate. In strong destination markets — Mallorca, the Côte d'Azur, Aspen, Lake Como — fractional shares have appreciated in line with whole-property appreciation in the same markets over the past five years. In weaker markets, the same is true in reverse: a fractional share is exposed to the same downside as a whole-property owner, just proportionally.

The mechanism: shares track the underlying property

When a buyer acquires a 1/8 share of a property-specific LLC, they own one-eighth of the equity in that LLC. The LLC's only meaningful asset is the home it holds. So when the home's market value rises, the LLC's equity value rises, and the buyer's share rises with it — pro-rata. The fractional wrapper doesn't change how the underlying real estate behaves; it just divides the participation.

This is the fundamental structural difference between fractional ownership and timeshare. A timeshare is a usage right that depreciates regardless of the underlying property's value. A fractional share is real-estate equity that tracks the underlying property's value in both directions.

What recent data shows

Across the destinations Co-Ownership Property monitors, fractional share prices on the resale market have broadly moved in step with the underlying local property market over the past five years. The pattern:

  • Mallorca, Costa del Sol, Côte d'Azur, Aspen: meaningful appreciation in fractional share values 2020–2025, tracking the strong rises in whole-property prices in the same markets.
  • French Alps: moderate appreciation, tracking the underlying ski-property market which has been less heated than the Mediterranean.
  • Some US resort markets: minor 2022–2023 softening as luxury second-home demand cooled, with recovery into 2024–2025.
  • Newer markets (Bali, parts of Mexico): too thin to read confidently — fractional supply is too recent to establish a meaningful resale baseline.

What affects the rate of appreciation

Five factors drive the rate at which a fractional share appreciates relative to the local whole-property market. First, the share's resale liquidity — strong operators with active resale pipelines often see share values track whole-property values closely; thin secondary markets can produce a discount. Second, the share's age in the market — shares that have established a clean resale precedent at a given price tend to appreciate more cleanly than first-generation listings. Third, the property's condition — homes that have been maintained to the original standard hold value better than those that have been under-invested in. Fourth, operator scale and reputation — well-known operators command a small premium on resale. Fifth, broader luxury second-home market conditions in the destination.

The realistic return profile

Fractional shares are not a high-yield investment product. The total return profile typically combines: capital appreciation tracking the underlying property (whatever the local market does); minor optional rental yield if the operator runs a rental programme on unused weeks; and the use-value of the personal-use weeks themselves, which is hard to quantify in pure financial terms but is what most buyers are really paying for.

Buyers who frame fractional purely as a financial investment usually undershoot expectations. Buyers who frame it as an asset-backed lifestyle purchase that participates in property appreciation usually find it delivers what was advertised.

What can erode share value

Three things can hurt share value independent of the underlying real estate. First, an operator's resale process falling behind — if owners can't find buyers efficiently, the implied discount on listed shares creates downward pressure. Second, a major unbudgeted repair or special assessment — this is usually covered by the reserve fund, but a particularly large assessment can dent the next year's market clearing price. Third, broader category sentiment — if the fractional category as a whole loses press goodwill, buyer interest can soften across all operators.

How to read the appreciation question for your own purchase

Two practical questions to ask: what has the underlying property market done in this destination over the past 5 years? And what has this operator's average resale price-vs-original done over the same period? The first sets your expectation for the underlying appreciation. The second tells you how cleanly the fractional structure has captured that appreciation in practice.
