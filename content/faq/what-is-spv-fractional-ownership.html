SPV in plain English

A special purpose vehicle is a legal entity created for one specific purpose — not a general-purpose company that owns many assets and runs many activities. In commercial law and finance, SPVs are used wherever a specific asset or activity needs to be ring-fenced legally and financially from a parent organisation. Real-estate funds use SPVs. Aviation finance uses SPVs. Securitisations use SPVs.

Does this describe what you buy at COP?

Not as such. The term describes one possible legal tool, not what you own. When you buy a co-ownership share through COP, you own a real share of the home — an eighth, a quarter — not a right to use it. You can sell it, pass it on to your heirs, and it rises and falls in value with the house.

The legal set-up behind each home differs by country and by property. It is arranged by our partner for each home and walked through with you before you buy, so you know exactly how your share is held and protected. We always recommend taking independent legal advice before you commit.

What to look for, whatever the set-up

  • Each home set up on its own, with its own group of co-owners, rather than pooled with other properties
  • The home's funds and reserve fund kept separate from the operator's own money
  • A clear co-ownership agreement covering usage, costs, decision-making and resale
  • A clear explanation of the legal set-up before you sign, reviewed by your own lawyer

What "SPV" doesn't mean

Two misconceptions worth clearing up. First, SPV doesn't mean offshore or tax-evasive — SPVs are standard legal structures used in all developed-market jurisdictions, transparent and tax-compliant. Second, SPV doesn't mean "small or hidden company" — many SPVs hold genuinely valuable assets and are well-regulated.
