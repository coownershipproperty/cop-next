What IMU is

IMU (Imposta Municipale Unica) is Italy's main municipal property tax. It applies annually based on the property's cadastral value (a regulated official value, typically lower than market value), with rates set by each comune (municipality) within a national framework. Second homes face higher rates than primary residences in most comuni. For luxury second-home properties in attractive destinations (Lake Como villages, Costa Smeralda, premium Tuscany comuni), IMU can run several thousand euros per year on the underlying property.

How IMU applies to fractional ownership

IMU is paid annually to the local comune on the property. The cost is built into the home's operating costs and recovered from owners through the annual fee — typically allocated pro-rata across all 8 owners.

Exactly who the IMU taxpayer is depends on the home's legal set-up, which our partner explains before you buy. In practice the operator typically handles the calculation and payment, so owners don't deal with it day to day; we recommend independent tax advice on your own position.

Cadastral value is typically 30-60% of market value, so the effective IMU rate on market value is roughly 0.3-0.5% per year. For a €2M Italian luxury property, that's €6,000-€10,000/year in IMU at the property level, allocated as €750-€1,250 per 1/8 share per year.

What the operator handles vs what owners handle

Operator handles: IMU calculation, annual filing with the comune, payment from the home's bank account, integration of the cost into the annual fee. Owners typically handle nothing directly on IMU — but should expect IMU to be a stated line item in the annual fee breakdown.

IMU vs the previous TASI / TARI structure

Italy's property tax structure was simplified in 2020 — IMU now combines what was previously IMU plus TASI (services tax). TARI (waste tax) remains separate and is paid annually at the property level alongside IMU. For fractional buyers, the consolidation simplified the picture; older operator documentation may reference TASI separately.

Foreign buyers share the IMU cost through the annual fee in the same way. Their home-country tax treatment depends on their country and the home's legal set-up, which our partner explains before you buy; treaty positions handle any double-taxation issues. We recommend independent advice.

What buyers should ask about IMU before purchase

Three questions. What is the specific IMU annual amount for this property (the operator should know to the euro)? What is the IMU rate trend in this comune over the past 3-5 years (some comuni have raised rates)? Is the IMU stated separately in the annual fee breakdown, or bundled into general "property taxes"?
