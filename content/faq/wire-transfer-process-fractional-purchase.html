The short answer: International wire to operator escrow or designated notary; typical 3-7 business days for major-bank transfers; first-time recipients can take longer; banks may flag large transfers for KYC review. Plan ahead: confirm bank arrangements at reservation, not at completion. Specialist FX providers (Wise Business, Revolut, OFX) typically deliver tighter FX rates than banks on large cross-border transfers. Most fractional purchases complete cleanly with proactive preparation.

Sources of FX margin and how to optimise

Three options for handling the FX conversion on a large cross-border purchase.

1. Your home bank's FX desk. Convenient — single bank handles everything. Margin: typically 1.5-3% above interbank rate for major currencies. On a €400k purchase, FX cost €6k-€12k.

2. Specialist FX provider (Wise Business, Revolut Business, OFX, Currencies Direct). More steps — fund the FX provider from your bank, FX provider converts and wires to operator. Margin: typically 0.3-0.8% above interbank for large transfers. On a €400k purchase, FX cost €1,200-€3,200 — saves €4k-€10k vs bank.

3. Forward contract through specialist FX provider. Lock the FX rate at reservation for the actual payment date 4-8 weeks later. Removes FX volatility during the cooling-off period. Small fee (0.1-0.3%) but useful for risk-averse buyers.

What banks may flag

Three triggers for additional bank review on the wire. First, large transfer size (typically €100k+) — banks routinely run additional KYC. Second, first-time recipient — banks verify the destination account. Three, source-of-funds documentation — banks may request documentation supporting where the funds came from (savings, property sale, investment liquidation).

None of these are blockers; they just add 3-7 days if not pre-arranged. Proactive preparation handles them smoothly.

What to do at reservation (not at completion)

Three things to set up at reservation rather than waiting until completion. First, contact your bank's international wire team — confirm the operator's accepting-bank details work; pre-authorise the transfer amount; provide source-of-funds documentation. Second, if using a specialist FX provider, set up the account and complete their KYC at reservation. Three, agree the FX-fixing approach (spot at payment date; forward contract; bank's FX desk).

Setting these up at reservation gives 4-8 weeks of buffer. Setting them up at completion creates last-minute pressure.

What the operator handles

Three operator-side items. Designating the escrow account (operator's solicitor or notary in property's country). KYC on the buyer to satisfy local regulatory requirements (overlaps with bank KYC). LLC member-register update once funds settle.

The completion sequence

Final-day mechanics. Funds settle in operator's escrow on completion date. Operator confirms receipt to buyer. LLC member-register updated within 1-2 business days. Buyer receives confirmation and share certificate. Booking platform access activated. First stay can be scheduled.

What buyers should ask

Four questions. What are the exact operator-bank-accepting details (IBAN/SWIFT)? What is the operator's preferred currency for receipt? Are there specific dates the operator can/cannot accept wires (some banks have month-end processing constraints)? How will the operator confirm receipt of funds to the buyer?
