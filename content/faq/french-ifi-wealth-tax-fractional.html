The short answer: French IFI (Impôt sur la Fortune Immobilière) is a wealth tax that applies above €1.3M of French real-estate value. For a non-resident buyer, a 1/8 fractional share of a €5M Côte d'Azur villa values at roughly €625k of French real estate — well below the IFI threshold. The same buyer owning the €5M villa outright would trigger IFI and face an annual charge in the tens of thousands. The fractional structure typically delivers this advantage automatically, though buyers with other French real estate need to aggregate carefully.

What IFI is

IFI is the French wealth tax on real estate. It replaced the broader French wealth tax (ISF) in 2018 and now applies only to real-estate assets. The threshold: net taxable French real-estate value above €1.3M. The rate is progressive, rising from 0.5% to 1.5% on the value above the threshold. For high-value French real estate, the annual charge can run into tens of thousands of euros.

How fractional ownership interacts with IFI

The deemed IFI value of a fractional share is calculated based on the underlying property value and the share size. For a 1/8 share of a €5M villa, the deemed value is roughly €625,000. For a buyer with no other French real estate, this sits well below the €1.3M threshold — IFI exposure is zero.

This is the structural reason fractional ownership has particular appeal at the top of the French luxury market. The €5M+ whole-property tier triggers IFI automatically; fractional ownership keeps individual buyers under the threshold.

The aggregation rule that matters

The €1.3M IFI threshold applies to the buyer's total French real estate, not to each property separately. A buyer with one French primary residence at €1M plus a 1/8 fractional share at €625k aggregates to €1.625M — above the threshold. The fractional structure helps but doesn't eliminate IFI exposure for buyers with substantial other French real estate.

The implication: if you already own French real estate, calculate the aggregated position before assuming the fractional share will be IFI-free.

French residents vs non-residents

For French residents, the IFI calculation includes worldwide real-estate values (with treaty adjustments). The fractional advantage still applies but is less dramatic because the rest of the resident's real-estate portfolio aggregates in.

For non-residents, only French real estate counts toward the IFI base. The fractional advantage is at its largest here — many non-resident buyers with no other French real estate face zero IFI exposure on a 1/8 share even of the most expensive French villas.

Other IFI rules buyers should know

The IFI base allows deductions for mortgages secured on the French real estate, and for documented refurbishment costs. The fractional share's debt position is typically zero (buyers usually pay cash), so the gross value applies. Annual filing is required even for non-residents above the threshold; the operator typically supports buyers with the basic reporting framework but specific tax filing is the buyer's responsibility.

What buyers should ask before purchase

Three questions for any French fractional purchase above €350k per 1/8 share. What is the operator's standard owner-side IFI handling support? What is the deemed French-RE value of the proposed share (request this in writing)? What is the buyer's full French real-estate picture today (existing primary residence, other holiday-home interests) so a proper IFI aggregation can be calculated?
