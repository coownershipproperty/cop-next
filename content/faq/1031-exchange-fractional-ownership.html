The short answer: Possible but structurally complex. IRS Section 1031 like-kind exchanges defer capital gains tax on the sale of investment real estate when proceeds are reinvested in equivalent property within defined timeframes. The applicability to fractional shares depends on multiple factors: how the home's legal set-up is classified for US federal tax purposes; whether the share is held for investment vs personal use; whether the replacement property qualifies as like-kind. Most fractional shares held primarily for personal use don't cleanly qualify. Some specifically-structured investment-purpose fractional holdings may. US specialist tax advice essential before relying on 1031 treatment.

What Section 1031 is

IRS Section 1031 allows US taxpayers to defer capital gains tax when they sell investment or business real estate and reinvest the proceeds in qualifying replacement property within strict timeframes (45 days to identify, 180 days to complete). Properly executed, a 1031 exchange defers federal capital gains tax that would otherwise be due on the sale.

Critically, post-2017 tax reform restricted Section 1031 to real property only — personal property and most other asset classes are no longer eligible. This affects the analysis of fractional shares meaningfully.

Why fractional ownership doesn't straightforwardly qualify

Three structural issues that complicate 1031 treatment for fractional shares:

1. Personal use vs investment use. Section 1031 requires the property to be held for productive use in trade or business or for investment. A fractional share used primarily for personal vacation use (the typical fractional buyer profile) doesn't cleanly fit either category. Property held for personal enjoyment is generally excluded from 1031 treatment.

2. Legal set-up classification. How a share is treated for US federal tax purposes depends on the country and the home's legal set-up, which our partner explains before you buy. Depending on that set-up, a share may or may not be treated as real property for 1031 purposes — partnership interests, for example, aren't 1031-eligible post-2017.

3. Like-kind requirement. Even if the share qualifies as real property, the replacement must be like-kind. Fractional shares from different operators with different structures, or replacement with whole real estate, may or may not meet the like-kind test depending on specific facts.

Where 1031 treatment might apply

Three narrow cases. First, fractional shares held by US-resident investors specifically for investment purposes (rental yield, not personal use) where the home's legal set-up supports real-estate classification. Second, certain TIC (tenancy-in-common) fractional structures that are recognised by IRS as direct real-estate ownership. Three, specialised fractional structures designed from the outset to support 1031 eligibility (uncommon in luxury vacation fractional; more common in commercial real-estate fractional).

Where 1031 treatment doesn't apply

The typical luxury vacation fractional buyer profile — buying a 1/8 share to use personally for ~45 days/year — doesn't qualify for 1031. The personal-use element disqualifies the share even before the legal set-up is considered.

The DST alternative

Delaware Statutory Trusts (DSTs) are sometimes used in commercial-real-estate fractional structures specifically to preserve 1031 eligibility. DSTs are pass-through entities that qualify as direct real-estate ownership for 1031 purposes when properly structured. DST structures are common in commercial fractional real estate (apartment buildings, retail properties) but very rare in luxury vacation fractional. If 1031 treatment is essential to a buyer's strategy, DST-structured commercial fractional may be a better fit than vacation fractional.

What US buyers should do

Three steps. First, don't assume 1031 will work for vacation-fractional purchases — it usually won't. Second, if 1031 is important, ask our partner how the home's legal set-up is treated for US tax purposes and about any personal-use vs investment-use elements. Three, engage a US tax attorney specifically familiar with 1031 and cross-border real estate before committing — generalist CPAs sometimes miss the specific 1031 mechanics.

The post-2017 reality for vacation fractional

For most US buyers of European or US-vacation fractional shares, 1031 is not part of the financial calculus. Capital gains on eventual disposal are typically taxed at long-term CGT rates (15-20% federal plus state) with foreign-tax credits where applicable. The disposal-tax position should be modelled directly rather than assumed to be deferrable.
