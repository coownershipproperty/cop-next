What you actually own

You own a real share of the home — an eighth, a quarter — not a right to use it. You can sell it, pass it on to your heirs, and it rises and falls in value with the house. That is the fundamental difference from a timeshare or a holiday club.

The legal set-up is arranged by our partner for each home and walked through with you before you buy. Because property law differs from one country to another, the way co-ownership is documented differs too — so there is no single answer to how ownership is recorded that holds for every home.

Why the set-up differs by home

Co-ownership homes sit in many countries — Spain, France, Italy, Portugal, the UK, the US, Mexico and beyond — each with its own property law, registration system and tax rules. Our partners arrange each home's legal set-up to work within the local rules, so that co-owners can share the home, sell or pass on their share, and make decisions together without friction.

The alternative — every co-owner buying and conveying slices of the property informally — produces enormous friction. Every share transfer becomes a complicated property sale, and decision-making between several owners has no agreed rules. A properly arranged co-ownership set-up avoids this.

Does this make your ownership less real?

No. Your share is yours: you can sell it, gift it, leave it in your will, and its value follows the value of the house. Big decisions — selling the home, major works — are decided together by the co-owners.

How to check your ownership

Before purchasing a share, ask our partner to walk you through three things:

  1. Exactly how your share of the home is held and documented for this specific property
  2. What documents you will receive on completion as evidence of your ownership
  3. How a future sale or transfer of your share works in practice

We also recommend having your own independent lawyer review the purchase documents before you sign.

What this means for tax filings

How tax works depends on the country and the home's legal set-up, which our partner explains before you buy. We recommend independent tax advice in your country of residence — and, where relevant, in the country where the home is located.
