The short answer: Co-ownership of a vacation home is a property structure where a small group of buyers, usually up to eight, each own a real share of one specific second home. A 1/8 share gives the owner around six weeks of use per year. "Co-ownership" is the term used most often in the UK and Europe; "fractional ownership" describes the same model in US usage.

Co-ownership and fractional ownership describe the same model

The two terms are used almost interchangeably across the European and US luxury second-home market. The differences are linguistic, not structural: UK and European buyers and operators tend to favour co-ownership, which reads as a more straightforward English description of the model. US buyers, operators and trade press tend to favour fractional ownership, which has been the standard term in the American resort markets since the late 1980s.

The model underneath the terminology is the same: one home, shared by a small number of owners who each own a real share of it, professional management, fair-rotation usage allocation.

What you actually own

You own a real share of the home — an eighth, a quarter — not a right to use it. You can sell it, pass it on to your heirs, and it rises and falls in value with the house. The legal set-up is arranged by our partner for each home and walked through with you before you buy.

Practically, this means: your share appears on your personal balance sheet as a real estate asset; it tracks the value of the underlying property; it can be sold, gifted or inherited; and it confers proportional voting rights in any decisions the co-owners make about the home.

How usage works

A 1/8 share entitles the owner to around six weeks of personal use a year — a little over six weeks. Most operators allocate this through a digital booking platform that rotates high-season, shoulder-season and off-season weeks fairly across all owners over a multi-year cycle. You don't get the same six weeks every year; you get a fair share of peak weeks over the course of your ownership.

Owners book specific dates in advance through the platform. Other co-owners are never at the property at the same time. When you're there, you have exclusive use of the entire home.

Why a formal set-up matters

Managed co-ownership differs from informal joint ownership in three important ways. First, a co-ownership agreement sets out the rules — usage, costs, decision-making — in writing. Second, resale is handled through our partner's resale process, without you having to sell the whole house. Third, professional management takes care of the home, so co-owners don't have to coordinate it themselves. How tax works depends on the country and the home's legal set-up, which our partner explains before you buy; we recommend independent advice.

Co-ownership vs timeshare vs holiday-let rental

Co-ownership is sometimes confused with timeshare. The distinction is fundamental: timeshare is a contractual right to use a property for a fixed period each year — you own time, not real estate. Co-ownership gives you a real share of the property itself. The first depreciates; the second tracks the underlying property value. Our fractional vs timeshare page covers the structural differences in detail.

Co-ownership also differs from holiday rentals or "fractional rental" memberships. Both are usage products with no equity component. Co-ownership owners participate in property appreciation; renters and members do not.

Where it's most established

Co-ownership is most established in Mediterranean Europe (Mallorca, Ibiza, the Costa del Sol, the Côte d'Azur, Tuscany, the Algarve), the European Alps (the French Alps, Verbier, the Dolomites), and across the US luxury-resort markets (Aspen, Lake Tahoe, Park City, Napa, Palm Springs). Mexico (Los Cabos), Bali and parts of the Caribbean are growing markets.
