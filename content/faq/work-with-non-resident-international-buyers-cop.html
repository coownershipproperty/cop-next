Why fractional ownership works particularly well for cross-border buyers

Three practical advantages over buying a whole foreign property.

1. Someone local does the work. The operator handles the home day to day — maintenance, bills, local taxes, insurance — so you don't need to manage a property remotely in another country.

2. The legal set-up is arranged for you. The legal set-up for each home is arranged by the operator and walked through with you before you buy. How tax works depends on the country and the home's legal set-up; we recommend independent cross-border advice.

3. Easier exit. Resale is handled through the operator's resale process, without you having to sell the whole house.

What the cross-border purchase process involves

Typical sequence for a cross-border fractional purchase:

  1. COP introduction — buyer connects with the operator through COP's marketplace
  2. Buyer KYC — passport, proof of address, source-of-funds documentation in line with the operator's standards
  3. Foreign tax IDs — Spanish NIE, French SIRET-equivalent, Italian codice fiscale, etc., obtained where required (operator facilitates)
  4. Cross-border tax advice — specialist advice on the buyer's home-country position vs the property-country position; operator can recommend cross-border specialists where useful
  5. Share-purchase agreement signed — reservation agreement plus final SPA
  6. International payment — typically by international wire to the operator's escrow account or local notary
  7. Share transfer — the share is transferred to the buyer on completion
  8. Onboarding and first stay — typical 6-12 weeks from initial introduction to first stay

What cross-border buyers should plan for

Three things to factor into the timeline. First, foreign tax IDs (NIE for Spain, codice fiscale for Italy) can take 4-8 weeks from initial application — start early. Second, international wire transfers between major banks typically take 3-7 business days but can take longer with first-time recipients — confirm bank arrangements early. Third, cross-border specialist tax advice should happen before signing the SPA, not after — leaves time to optimise the holding vehicle.
