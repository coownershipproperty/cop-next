The complexity isn't structural — it's tax: the gift triggers gift-tax obligations in the property's country, the giver's home country, and potentially the recipient's country, depending on relevant double-taxation treaties. The mechanical process

Gifting a fractional share to a family member is a documented LLC membership transfer. The steps:

  1. Notify the operator's owner-services team of the intent to gift
  2. Operator provides the gift-transfer paperwork (varies by LLC's jurisdiction)
  3. Recipient completes identification and tax-residency declarations
  4. Documentation is executed (typically with a notary in the property's country)
  5. LLC membership register updated to reflect the new owner
  6. Recipient receives confirmation and full access to booking and ownership rights

Mechanical timeline: 4–6 weeks. Operator legal fees: typically €500–€2,000. Lower for direct-family transfers in jurisdictions with streamlined processes.

The structural advantage over gifting whole property

Gifting a whole foreign property typically requires: a notary in the property's country, transfer-tax payment, change to the land registry, and potentially a fresh round of due diligence and legal documentation in the recipient's name. The complete process can take 3–6 months and cost tens of thousands of euros in fees and taxes.

Gifting a fractional share involves transferring an LLC membership interest. The underlying property doesn't change hands — only the LLC's owner ledger updates. Most of the friction and cost of a whole-property gift is structurally absent.

Gift-tax considerations

Gift tax applies in three potential places:

  • The property's country: the SCI/SL/SRL/LLC change of member is taxable in many jurisdictions. Direct-line transfers (parent to child) typically benefit from large allowances.
  • The giver's home country: the giver may be liable for home-country gift tax. UK gift tax applies if the giver dies within 7 years (PET rules); US gift tax applies above annual exclusions and the lifetime exemption.
  • The recipient's home country: some jurisdictions tax gifts received from abroad; others don't.

The fractional structure usually makes the gift smaller in absolute value (one share rather than a whole property), which can keep it below relevant thresholds in all three places.

Worked example: UK parent gifting to UK adult child

A UK-domiciled parent gifts a 1/8 share of a French villa (deemed value €400,000) to their adult child. The mechanical paperwork at the operator is straightforward. Tax position:

  • France: SCI member transfer; direct-line allowance of €100,000 per child per 15 years applies; net gift base €300,000 taxed under French rules (~20% on this band) = ~€60,000 French gift tax, paid by recipient
  • UK: Potentially Exempt Transfer (PET); if parent survives 7 years, no UK IHT charge; if parent dies within 7 years, taper relief applies

Exact rates depend on the specific calendar year, applicable allowances, and treaty positions. Always model with a specialist before completing.

Partial gifts

You don't have to gift the whole share. A buyer with multiple shares can gift one share and keep others. A buyer with a single share can gift a portion of it (e.g. 50% of the 1/8 share) — though this creates a co-ownership of the share between you and the recipient, which is administratively more complex than a full transfer.

What to verify before gifting

Three questions. Does the LLC operating agreement permit gift transfers, and under what conditions (some operators require approval; most don't for direct family)? What is the operator's specific paperwork process and fee for gift transfers? What is the cross-border gift-tax picture for the specific giver-recipient combination?
