The short answer: Twelve baseline questions. (1) Is the property in a property-specific LLC verifiable on the corporate registry? (2) Can I read the operating agreement before signing? (3) What is the operator's average days-to-resale on completed resales in past 24 months? (4) What is the operator's special-assessment history on this property over past 5-10 years? (5) What is the LLC's current reserve fund balance vs target? (6) What is the annual fee history over past 5 years? (7) What is the rotation schedule for the next 8 years? (8) What is the cooling-off period in this SPA? (9) Can I speak to existing owners? (10) What home-country tax position applies to me? (11) What is the operator's commission on resale? (12) What happens if the operator goes out of business?

The twelve questions — and what good answers look like

1. Is the property in a property-specific LLC?

Good answer: yes, named LLC with registration number you can verify on the relevant corporate registry. Bad answer: vague or evasive — possibly shared corporate vehicle, which carries higher structural risk.

2. Can I read the operating agreement before signing?

Good answer: yes, here it is, please review with your lawyer. Bad answer: "we'll share at signing" — you need to review before committing, not after.

3. What is the operator's average days-to-resale over past 24 months?

Good answer: specific number with track-record evidence (e.g. "98 days average across 47 closed resales in past 24 months"). Bad answer: vague claims about "quick resale" without specifics.

4. What is the operator's special-assessment history?

Good answer: specific record showing zero or small assessments over recent years, with explanation of any. Bad answer: deflection or vague claims about "rare" assessments.

5. What is the LLC's current reserve fund balance vs target?

Good answer: specific current balance and target (€X balance vs €Y target per the operator's capital plan). Bad answer: vague claims about "well-funded" without numbers.

6. What is the annual fee history over past 5 years?

Good answer: year-by-year specific percentages (e.g. 2022: +4%; 2023: +6%; 2024: +5%; 2025: +5%; 2026: +4%). Bad answer: vague claims about "moderate increases" without specifics.

7. What is the rotation schedule for the next 8 years?

Good answer: specific schedule showing peak-week priority for each owner for each year of the 8-year cycle. Bad answer: vague claims about "fair rotation" without the actual schedule.

8. What is the cooling-off period in this SPA?

Good answer: specific days from signing during which the buyer can withdraw without penalty (typically 7-14 days in EU). Bad answer: shorter than statutory minimum, or unclear withdrawal mechanism.

9. Can I speak to existing owners?

Good answer: yes, here's a structured process to connect with willing existing owners. Bad answer: refusal — may indicate operator doesn't want owner feedback shared with prospects.

10. What home-country tax position applies to me?

Good answer: operator suggests engaging specialist; provides general framework; doesn't pretend to advise on personal tax. Bad answer: detailed personal tax advice from operator without specialist input — operators aren't tax advisers.

11. What is the operator's commission on resale?

Good answer: specific percentage (typically 3-6%) with breakdown of what it covers. Bad answer: vague or significantly above 6%.

12. What happens if the operator goes out of business?

Good answer: explains the property-specific LLC structure protects underlying ownership; describes replacement-of-management mechanism in operating agreement; provides reassurance about operator's own financial position. Bad answer: dismisses the question or provides vague reassurance without structural explanation.

How to use the questions

Send the list to the operator in writing during your due-diligence period and ask for written responses. Written answers are easier to compare across operators and harder to retract later. Most credible operators are happy to provide written answers; operators reluctant to do so are a meaningful warning sign.

What good answers across all 12 indicate

Specific numerical answers across all questions, willingness to share documentation, structured processes for owner-to-prospective-buyer connection, and recommendation of specialist tax advice — together signal a credible, well-run operator. Vague answers, refusal to share documentation, dismissal of any of the questions all indicate elevated risk that warrants either more due diligence or walking away.
