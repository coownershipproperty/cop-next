## 1. Pacaso

### 1. Company identity
- **Legal/brand:** Pacaso Inc. (the LLCs that hold individual properties are property-specific subsidiaries).
- **Founded:** October 2020.
- **HQ:** San Francisco, California.
- **Co-founders:** Austin Allison (CEO; founder of Dotloop, sold to Zillow Group) and Spencer Rascoff (Executive Chairman; former CEO of Zillow, co-founder of Hotwire). Allison and Rascoff worked together when Dotloop was acquired by Zillow.
- **Employees:** Pacaso self-describes a multi-hundred headcount across product, real estate ops, and concierge; specific 2026 headcount not disclosed in the public press round-up.

### 2. Funding
- **Total equity raised:** ~$300M+ disclosed cumulative as of October 2025 (after the Reg A+ closing).
- **Headline rounds:**
  - Mar 2021 Series B: $75M, valuation ~$1B (unicorn at 5 months).
  - Sep 2021 Series C: $125M led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2 at $1.5B valuation. Co-investors: Fifth Wall, Greycroft, Maveron, Crosscut Ventures, 75 & Sunny Ventures, Global Founders Capital, Gaingels. Howard Schultz is named as an angel.
  - Jul 2025: $35M as initial Reg A+ tranche from ~10,000 retail investors.
  - Oct 2025: Closes oversubscribed Reg A+ at $72.5M total from 17,500+ retail investors — described as the largest real-estate Reg A+ of 2025.
- **Ticker reserved:** NASDAQ:PCSO (reserved May 2025; no formal IPO filed as of this report).
- **Profitability:** Net loss $31.4M in 2024. $164.5M gross real-estate transacted (excl. whole-home sales). Adjusted gross profit $23.6M (+18% YoY). EBITDA loss reduced 24%.
- Note: Tracxn shows a higher cumulative figure ($1.669B over 13 rounds) that appears to include debt facilities and asset-level financing, not just equity. Use the ~$300M equity figure when comparing apples-to-apples with MYNE/Vivla.

### 3. Geographic coverage
- **US:** ~30+ US destinations across California (Lake Tahoe, Napa/Sonoma, Palm Springs, Malibu), Florida, Hawaii, Colorado (Aspen, Breckenridge, Telluride, Vail), the Carolinas, Texas, Utah.
- **International:** UK (London, Cotswolds area), France (Paris, Cap Ferrat / Côte d'Azur, Provence), Spain (Marbella as the first 2021 launch, plus Ibiza, Mallorca), Mexico (Cabo San Lucas, Punta Mita), and as of Jun 2025 — Italy (Florence, Milan, Rome).
- **Self-claim:** "Operations in more than 40 destinations."
- **Owner count:** 1,500+ in mid-2024, 2,000+ by 2025 (Pacaso PR; SherpaReport).
- COP catalog of 190 is the largest single-partner pool, dominated by US listings.

### 4. Property profile
- Positioning: **luxury single-family homes**, typical sold price $1M–$10M whole-home equivalent (so a 1/8 share is typically $150k–$1.5M+).
- Property types: predominantly single-family villas; recent Italian expansion adds apartments/penthouses in Florence, Milan, Rome.
- Curation philosophy: Pacaso buys the home directly (not just listing referrals), performs light renovation, then re-lists in fractions. This is materially different from listing-aggregator competitors.

### 5. Share structure
- **Default:** 1/8 share = up to 44 nights/year (Pacaso's own materials; equivalent to ~6 weeks).
- Max consecutive stay per visit: 14 nights.
- Owners may buy multiple shares (up to 4/8 = half) per Pacaso. Buying multiple shares proportionally increases nights and stay length.
- 8 owners maximum per property.

### 6. Legal structure
- **US:** Each Pacaso home is held by a property-specific Delaware-or-state LLC. Each fractional buyer receives a 1/8 membership interest in that LLC. The LLC is named on the deed. Per Pacaso PR: "shares ranging from 1/8 to 1/2."
- **France:** SCI (Société Civile Immobilière) — the canonical French civil-law property holding company. Pacaso confirms it "partners with leading...legal experts" but does not provide owner-facing legalese for SCI shares.
- **Italy / Spain / UK:** "Local equivalent structure" — exact entity per country is not published on Pacaso public pages; verify on a per-listing basis. For Spain this would typically be a Sociedad Limitada (SL); for the UK, a limited company.
- St. Helena, CA settlement (2024) requires Pacaso to register St. Helena LLC interests as fractional and prohibits further fractional expansion in that city beyond the four existing homes — a precedent for tighter US municipal oversight.

### 7. Pricing transparency
- Listing pages on pacaso.com show share price, but not the full underlying whole-home price unless you compute 8× share price. Critics (Real Deal Apr 2026) note that a Hawaii listing showed an implied markup of ~32.5% over local comparables — vs the official 12% markup Pacaso markets.
- Pacaso self-discloses a **12% one-time service fee** on initial share purchase. This is on top of the actual property value.
- Annual operating fees not displayed on the public listing — disclosed during the sales process.

### 8. Annual fees model
- Monthly fee covers: property management, routine maintenance, utilities, taxes, insurance, reserve fund. Each home's budget is "approved by homeowners annually and reconciled quarterly" (Pacaso FAQ).
- A separate **$99/month technology fee** per share is charged for the SmartStay app and platform access.
- No fixed % is published — but Trustpilot complaints reference assessments around $8K/owner after a year due to budget shortfalls, and at least one review cites a **65% monthly fee increase** in year 2 — consistent with cost-pass-through rather than fixed-rate billing.
- Pacaso states publicly: "Pacaso has not raised property management fee tiers over the past two years," referring to the technology/management margin (not the cost-passthrough portion of the budget).

### 9. Resale process
- Operator-supported. Pacaso lists the share to:
  1. Existing Pacaso owners (internal marketplace),
  2. Pacaso's prospect pool,
  3. Open market via Pacaso's site.
- **Commission:** 6% on resale (vs the 12% markup on primary). Buyers pay no title/inspection/service fees on resale.
- **Holding rule:** Seller must retain at least one of the eight slots — i.e., you cannot simultaneously sell all your shares back through the platform if you own more than one. (Frequently cited Trustpilot complaint.)
- **Average time on market:** 99 days (Pacaso self-reported, vs 120–200+ for $5M–$10M whole-home luxury comps).
- **Resale appreciation claim:** ~10% historical average (Pacaso PR).
- ROFR: Pacaso retains right of first offer/refusal in some agreements; verify per-listing.

### 10. Mortgage / financing
- **70% LTV maximum** through Pacaso's integrated lender network. 30% down payment required.
- Pacaso self-guarantees payment to lender if a buyer defaults — risk mitigation for the bank.
- Named lender partners not disclosed publicly (Pacaso uses "banking partners"). Pacaso earns origination fees on the loan.
- No PMI (30% down removes need).

### 11. Cooling-off / buyer protection
- Pacaso does not publicly advertise a statutory cooling-off period; in the US, real estate purchase contracts are governed by state law and the SEC Reg A+ filings, not by the federal 3-day cooling-off rule for door-to-door sales.
- Buyers receive an LLC operating agreement and PSA; specific 7-day or 14-day right of rescission is **not consistently documented** in public-facing Pacaso material. Unverified — recommend per-state confirmation.

### 12. Rental program
- **Pacaso explicitly prohibits owner rentals**. No Airbnb, no Vrbo, no commercial use. Pacaso "proactively monitors short-term rental sites to find violations."
- This is a deliberate distinction from competitors like Ember, Ancana, and Vivla, all of which allow or facilitate rentals of unused weeks.
- Practical implication: a Pacaso share generates lifestyle value and potential capital appreciation, but **zero ongoing income**.

### 13. Scheduling / calendar
- **SmartStay scheduling app** (proprietary).
- Rolling 24-month booking window.
- "Fairness logic": every owner gets one peak-season stay before anyone can book a second. Stay length capped at 14 nights consecutive.
- Bookings made the way owners "already book travel" — calendar-style UI.

### 14. Concierge / management scope
- **Dedicated local Home Manager** per property.
- Pre-arrival inspection. Linens, towels, paper goods, kitchen essentials all restocked. "Turkish towels, premium kitchen products, porcelain tableware" — Pacaso's published service promise.
- Cleaning, turnover, vendor management, recurring + preventive maintenance, financial oversight all included.
- Among the most operationally heavy/full-service of the 5 partners.

### 15. Press / reputation
- **Major positive coverage:** Inman (Best of Proptech 2024 winner for co-founders), Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Goldman Sachs Private Wealth Network (Pacaso PDF circulated).
- **Major negative/controversial coverage:**
  - *The Real Deal* (Apr 2026): "Pacaso's fractional ownership model under scrutiny by customers" — explicitly discusses incentive misalignment, hidden markups, financial-disclosure questions during Reg A+.
  - *NPR Planet Money* (2021): "Pacaso Is Turning Houses Into Corporations."
  - *Vice* (2022): "Tech 'Unicorn' Pacaso Is Turning Homes Into LLCs and Pissing Off the Neighbors."
  - *Napa Valley Register* extensive coverage of St. Helena lawsuit (resolved 2024; Pacaso permanently capped at 4 St. Helena homes).
- **Community opposition groups spawned:** Communities Against Timeshares (CATs, Sonoma), STOP (Sonomans Together Opposing Pacaso), NOPE (Neighborhoods Opposing Pacaso Encroachment, St Helena), similar groups in Utah, Texas, Maui, NY.

### 16. Buyer reviews
- Trustpilot: predominantly positive on customer service, scheduling app UX, and the buy-side experience.
- Recurring complaints (Trustpilot, Real Deal, FastExpert):
  - Year-2 budget shortfalls / unexpected assessments.
  - "Must retain one share" resale rule.
  - Resale support reportedly weak post-sale.
  - Owners cannot fire Pacaso as listing agent.
  - Some claim "arbitrary rule changes."

### 17. USP — what makes Pacaso different
**The biggest, best-funded, most operationally polished co-ownership operator in the world — and the only one with serious US density. They buy and renovate the property themselves, take a 12% one-time markup, run a proprietary scheduling app, prohibit rentals (purely lifestyle, not income), and target the top of the luxury market. But the scale and US-municipal pushback are unique to them.**

## 2. MYNE Homes

### 1. Company identity
- **Legal/brand:** MYNE Homes GmbH.
- **Founded:** 2021.
- **HQ:** Berlin, Germany.
- **Co-founders:** Nikolaus Thomale (Managing Director — law background, Bucerius Law School Hamburg + Tel Aviv; founder of Jura-Online, sold to Otto Schmidt in 2017) and Fabian Löhmer (WHU Vallendar + Kobe Daigaku Japan; previously sold a startup to Mediengruppe RTL Deutschland in 2015). Founding team also includes Moritz Vossenberg (CTO) and Philipp Wagmann (real estate). Public reports vary on which 2 vs 4 names are listed as "co-founders"; Thomale and Löhmer are the consistent face of the company.
- **Origin story:** Founders inspired by Thomale's underused Swedish family holiday home.
- **Employees:** Approx 50–100 (LinkedIn / press estimate; not officially disclosed).

### 2. Funding
- **Total raised:** €60M+ equity + debt.
- **Headline rounds:**
  - Jul 2022: €23.5M seed (one of Europe's largest seed rounds in proptech) led by Scope Hanson and Motive Ventures.
  - Aug 2024: €40M Series A — equity + debt — led by Limestone Capital AG, with Motive Ventures and Scope Hanson re-upping. Described in trade press as the **largest European proptech round of 2024** at time of announcement.
- **Valuation:** Not publicly disclosed.

### 3. Geographic coverage
- **Confirmed countries:** Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain, France, Sweden, Croatia (added 2024). UK launch announced in 2025.
- **Hot destinations:** Mallorca, Ibiza, Tyrol (Austria), Rügen + Bavaria (Germany), Liguria (Italy), Côte d'Azur (France), Värmdö (Sweden).
- **Property count:** "High three-digit number of shares" by mid-2024 (Tech.eu). MYNE self-describes as the European market leader for managed co-ownership.
- **Expansion roadmap:** Benelux + UK announced. Portugal mentioned as future.
- COP catalog: 95 MYNE listings — second-largest pool on the COP site, suggesting MYNE is the most actively-sold European brand in COP's mix.

### 4. Property profile
- Positioning: **premium European holiday homes** — fincas in Mallorca/Ibiza, alpine chalets in Tyrol, villas in Liguria/Côte d'Azur, archipelago homes in Sweden.
- Equity contributions start at €50,000 (per MYNE site). Typical 1/8 share €100k–€500k; some listings up to €999k for 1/8 (Mallorca/Ibiza premium).
- Property types: villa, apartment, penthouse, finca, chalet, townhouse, semi-detached.
- Curation philosophy: managed co-ownership of "premium" holiday homes; not ultra-luxury (Pacaso) nor mid-market.

### 5. Share structure
- 1/2 to 1/8 share. Up to 8 owners per property; typically 8.
- Buyer can own multiple shares per property.
- Per share usage rights expand proportionally (MYNE doesn't publish a fixed nights/year per share publicly, but the booking system enforces equitable rotation).

### 6. Legal structure
- **Spain:** A registered company holds the property title in Spain's Registro de la Propiedad. Purchasers buy shares in that company — they are shareholders / co-owners (typically an SL — Sociedad Limitada). MYNE blog confirms shares are entered in the land registry.
- **Germany:** Typically GbR (Gesellschaft bürgerlichen Rechts) or specialized partnership; not separately documented in published material.
- **France/Italy/Austria/Sweden/Croatia:** Local equivalent SPV structures. MYNE describes itself as the property "trustee/manager" rather than the title-holder.
- MYNE emphasizes that co-owners are "real property owners with shares officially recorded in the land registry" — explicitly to contrast with timeshare.

### 7. Pricing transparency
- Each listing on myne-homes.com shows the share price + the implied whole-property value (typically displayed as "from €X" with link to the brochure).
- Annual fees: described qualitatively as "shared monthly costs" with consumption-based + flat components, but **no public % range** on the website. Verify on a per-listing basis (this is similar to Pacaso).
- Brochures sent post-enquiry contain the full fee breakdown.

### 8. Annual fees model
- 3-bucket structure:
  1. Fixed shared costs (insurance, taxes, MYNE management fee, reserve fund),
  2. Variable shared costs (allocated by usage — utilities, cleaning between stays),
  3. Consumption-based personal costs.
- Budget set per property, approved by co-owners, reconciled at year-end.
- MYNE has not published a fixed % of fraction value. **Industry norm for premium co-ownership is 1.5%–4% of fraction value per annum** — MYNE almost certainly falls in this band, but this should be verified per listing.

### 9. Resale process
- **Operator-supported.** MYNE Co-Owners "can easily sell their share at market value."
- MYNE markets the share to its existing prospect pool. Buyer is vetted (lifestyle fit with remaining co-owners).
- **Specific resale timeline and commission % not published publicly** by MYNE. This is a gap vs Vivla (which publishes ~4 weeks avg + +11% appreciation) and Pacaso (99 days + 6% commission). Verify in MYNE's terms.

### 10. Mortgage / financing
- MYNE confirms "many banks grant mortgages to the fractional ownership mechanism" but does **not name an exclusive lender partner** in public materials.
- Other co-owners must accept the credit terms but are not obligated to be holders of the loan.
- LTV ranges are not standardized; treated case-by-case with the buyer's bank.

### 11. Cooling-off / buyer protection
- Not specifically documented in public MYNE materials. EU consumer law typically provides a statutory 14-day cooling-off period for off-premises real estate intermediation — but pure real estate purchase contracts often fall outside this rule. **Verify in the per-country purchase contract.**

### 12. Rental program
- MYNE allows rental of unused weeks. Rental income is pooled and applied to reduce co-owners' monthly fees (mechanism similar to Vivla).
- This is the opposite of Pacaso's prohibition.
- Specific revenue split % (operator vs. owners) not publicly published.

### 13. Scheduling / calendar
- MYNE app handles bookings.
- Booking window: **2 days to 2 years in advance**.
- Fairness logic: if you take Christmas one year, other co-owners get first priority for the next.
- Reservations are automatic, transparent, and visible to all co-owners.

### 14. Concierge / management scope
- MYNE handles management, servicing, and maintenance. Owners "maintain day-to-day control."
- Cleaning between stays, vendor coordination, regulatory compliance (tourist licenses where applicable), and accounting.
- Less aggressively branded "concierge" than Pacaso, but functionally similar.

### 15. Press / reputation
- **Major coverage:** EU-Startups, Sifted, Tech.eu, Silicon Canals, Skift, Rolling Stone UK (sponsored/branded content but published), Financial Times (referenced in broader second-home market piece), PhocusWire, ShortTermRentalz, Maddyness UK.
- **Controversies:** No major scandals found. MYNE has avoided the US-style municipal pushback because European zoning regimes already permit fractional/share-based ownership in most countries.
- Trustpilot rating: ~4.7 stars from ~100+ reviewers (positive transparency, helpful onboarding, smooth process).

### 16. Buyer reviews
- Trustpilot: 4.7/5. Common praise: transparent communication, smooth onboarding, helpful team.
- Glassdoor: limited public employee reviews.
- Less mature review base than Pacaso (smaller customer pool, younger company).

### 17. USP — what makes MYNE different
**Europe's broadest country footprint (7+ countries) with a Berlin-based GmbH structure that's easy for German/DACH and Nordic buyers to trust. Allows owner rentals to offset annual fees — a major contrast with Pacaso. Premium-but-accessible price band (entry from €50k for a fraction). Best fit for buyers who want a Spanish/Mallorcan/Alpine home and want to actively rent it.**

## 3. Vivla

### 1. Company identity
- **Legal/brand:** VIVLA LIFESTYLE S.L. (Spanish Sociedad Limitada at the holding-company level).
- **Founded:** 2021.
- **HQ:** Madrid, Spain.
- **Co-founders:**
  - **Carlos Emilio Gómez** (CEO) — 10 years at Google across EU + LATAM roles. Co-founded 7r, Spanish corporate venture builder (9 companies built; 3 exits).
  - **Carlos Floria** (COO) — Banking (Société Générale CIB, RBS) and VC (Inveready Technology Investment Group). Led debt funds + ventures.
  - **Iván Rodríguez** (CRO) — co-founder of Parclick (sold to Italian FAAC group in 2019).
- **Employees:** ~54 (Crunchbase data, July 2025).

### 2. Funding
- **Total raised 2025 alone:** €64M.
- **Headline rounds:**
  - Mar 2022: €26M pre-seed (debt + equity), led by Samaipata.
  - Jul 2025: €8M equity round led by Samaipata + Bonsai Partners, FJ Labs, Extension Fund + strategics Andbank, Kronos, Stoneweg, Concrete VC. Extended in Dec 2025 with €1M from existing VIVLA homeowners.
  - 2025: Senior debt facility expanded to **€50M by Fasanara Capital** (asset-backed real estate credit facility) + €5M venture debt tranche from Extension Fund — bringing total debt-equity package to €55M+.
- **Valuation:** Not publicly disclosed.
- Vivla is heavily debt-financed because it warehouses properties before reselling them as fractions — debt is structurally important to the model.

### 3. Geographic coverage
- **Spain-only.** Current destinations: Ibiza, Menorca, Baqueira (ski), Cádiz, Costa Blanca, Costa de la Luz, Madrid, Cantabria, Formentera, Costa Brava, Costa del Sol.
- **2026 roadmap (per Idealista, Mar 2026):** Asturias, Canary Islands, Costa del Sol expansion, Formigal (ski), Mallorca.
- **Property count:** 63 properties as of Nov 2025 (Idealista). Target: 100 by end of 2026. **Note**: COP catalog shows 34, suggesting COP carries about half of Vivla's portfolio.
- **In Baqueira specifically, co-ownership now accounts for 5% of the prime ski market** (Idealista Nov 2025) — Vivla is the leader.

### 4. Property profile
- Positioning: **premium Spanish second homes** with destination diversity from beach to ski.
- Property types: villa, apartment (Madrid), chalet (Baqueira and other ski).
- COP-detected feature taxonomy: pool, sea-views, garden, terrace, fireplace.
- Curation philosophy: 8-fraction luxury homes targeting Spanish + EU buyers seeking premium second homes for personal use + some rental.

### 5. Share structure
- Default 1/8 (8 fractions per property).
- **Max 4 fractions per person** (50% cap — explicit on Vivla's how-it-works page).
- 42 days/year per 1/8 share.
- Distribution differs between beach homes (peak summer) and mountain homes (peak ski) so each owner gets equitable peak access.

### 6. Legal structure
- **Spanish SL (Sociedad Limitada) per property.** The SL holds 100% of the property. Co-owners are SL shareholders. Vivla manages the SL operationally.
- Each owner signs a partnership agreement at notary; shares are notarial deeds with full legal property rights (Vivla emphasizes "real, indivisible part of a property with notarial deed").
- This is the same structure as MYNE-in-Spain and &Hamlet-in-Spain — a Spanish-law convention rather than a Vivla innovation.

### 7. Pricing transparency
- Vivla publishes share price + implied whole-home value on each listing page.
- "Everything included" in purchase price: stamp duty, ~12–14% Spanish transaction costs, furniture, design, SL setup, legal fees.
- Annual fees are publicly stated as a percentage range (see #8).

### 8. Annual fees model
- **Vivla publicly states 1.5%–2% of fraction price per year** for maintenance (Vivla scheduling FAQ). However, earlier Vivla blog content cited 2%–2.5%, and some 2024–2025 third-party content cites 3.5%–4%. Range therefore depends on house. **For a €100k 1/8 share, monthly running cost is on the order of €200–€350**.
- Includes: taxes, community/HOA fees, utilities, maintenance, cleaning.
- Rental income (when owners opt to rent) is applied directly to reduce monthly fees — material offset.

### 9. Resale process
- **Operator-supported.** Existing co-owners get first right of refusal; if they decline, owner is free to sell to any vetted buyer.
- **Average resale time: <4 weeks** in 2025 (Vivla Resale & Liquidity Guide, corroborated by Idealista Mar 2026).
- **Average resale appreciation: +11%** in 2025.
- 60%+ of co-owners have rented or exchanged their shares (Vivla self-reported).
- Trustpilot includes one outlier complaint alleging non-refund — single critical voice among broader positives.

### 10. Mortgage / financing
- Vivla works with multiple Spanish banks. **No public exclusive lender partnership** with CaixaBank or others verified.
- The model accepts that co-owners can take mortgages individually; the SL share is collateral. Other co-owners must accept the credit terms but are not co-borrowers.
- Pre-seed press release referenced support for ~70% LTV financing in Spain. **The user's CLAUDE.md mentions "Vivla reportedly does 100% financing" — this was not verified by third-party sources. Treat as unverified / sales-rep-level claim.**

### 11. Cooling-off / buyer protection
- Spanish real estate purchase contracts generally do not carry a federal statutory cooling-off period (unlike consumer goods). However, deposits paid before the notarial deed (arras) can be legally structured as refundable (arras penitenciales) or non-refundable (arras confirmatorias) — owner protection is contract-dependent. Not publicly documented as a Vivla-specific buyer right.

### 12. Rental program
- **Vivla actively rents owner shares**. Vivla handles tourist licensing, listing on Airbnb-style platforms, screening, and concierge.
- 65% of bookings come from Vivla's internal client network.
- Average property occupancy: 80%.
- Rental income credited to owner's monthly fees automatically.
- **Specific operator commission/revenue split not publicly disclosed.**

### 13. Scheduling / calendar
- Annual calendar opens in September for the following calendar year.
- 42 days/year per share, with a fairness rotation across peak weeks.
- Owners can also book last-minute (when available).
- App-based, with auto-block when rented to outside guests.

### 14. Concierge / management scope
- Integral property management. From tourist license acquisition to property prep to customer experience monitoring.
- Mid-stay maintenance handled by Vivla.
- Spanish-language home managers in destination markets.

### 15. Press / reputation
- **Major coverage:** EU-Startups, Idealista, Tech.eu, La Voz de Ibiza, CB Insights, AIM Group, Novobrief, Capital-Riesgo, PitchBook.
- No major scandal or community-pushback story analogous to Pacaso's St Helena situation. Spanish municipal regimes are not as resistant to fractional models.
- Resale data is positively cited by Spanish trade press as evidence that co-ownership "brings new liquidity" to Spain's holiday-home market.

### 16. Buyer reviews
- Trustpilot: mixed but lean positive. Owners cite smooth process, helpful team, clear documentation, peace of mind.

- Operating expenses passed through at cost (no &Hamlet markup):

  - Cleaning between stays (invoiced per use)
  - Municipal fees + insurance (1/8 each)
  - Electricity + utilities (by usage)
  - Property management — accounting, vendor coordination
