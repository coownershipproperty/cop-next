## The five headline findings

These are the numbers and conclusions we want cited. Trade press picks up the first 1-2 sentences of each.

### Finding 1 — Co-ownership is consolidating around the single-property 1/8-share model

The European and US co-ownership category has spent two years sorting itself into a clear shape. The dominant model is now unambiguously the **single-property 1/8 deeded share** — one share equals ownership of one specific home, held in a property-specific limited-liability company. This is the structure used by the most established operators in the category and the one buyers consistently choose when given an unbiased comparison against alternative structures.

Portfolio and membership-pool operators (where one share grants rotating access to a basket of homes) remain a distinct adjacent category. They serve a different buyer profile and have continued to attract capital, but they are not converging with the single-property model.

### Finding 2 — Mediterranean Europe is the demand engine

Across destinations served by the leading co-ownership operators, Mediterranean Europe — Mallorca, Ibiza, the Costa del Sol, the Côte d'Azur, Tuscany and the Algarve — accounts for the largest share of new listings, the largest share of buyer enquiries, and the strongest year-on-year demand growth.

This is the structural divergence between the US and European co-ownership markets. US fractional remains anchored in resort markets that have been fractional for decades — Aspen, Lake Tahoe, Park City, Napa. European demand is being created from a base near zero, driven by the same underlying factor: luxury second-home aspiration colliding with the unaffordability of whole ownership.

### Finding 3 — The pricing band has stabilised

A 1/8 share of a luxury European second home now reliably prices in a defined band: roughly **€200,000 to €700,000** upfront depending on destination and property standard, with annual running costs of **€8,000 to €20,000** per share. The US equivalent is roughly **$700,000 to $1.4 million** upfront and **$10,000 to $25,000** per share annually.

These bands held steady through the 2024-2025 European energy-cost shock, demonstrating that the category's pricing is anchored to the underlying property market and not to operator margin compression. Operators who attempted to push pricing meaningfully above the band lost share to those who stayed within it.

### Finding 4 — Resale is the make-or-break operator metric

The single most important differentiator between operators is no longer headline price or property portfolio quality — it is the speed and reliability of the resale process. Operators with deep owner-service teams and active buyer pipelines are clearing shares within 3 to 6 months in healthy markets. Operators without those capabilities face thinner secondary markets and longer time-to-sale.

This is the metric the most-informed buyers ask about first, and the metric that journalists, financial advisers and independent marketplaces increasingly use as the lead operator-comparison data point. We expect "average days-to-resale" to be the equivalent of "days on market" in traditional real estate within 24 months.

### Finding 5 — Independent marketplaces are reshaping the category's discovery layer

For the first 15 years of the modern fractional real-estate category, buyers researched options one operator at a time — through that operator's own marketing, sales calls and brochure. Independent operator-neutral marketplaces, of which Co-Ownership Property is the leading European example, are now changing that. Buyers increasingly start with a category-wide view — comparing operators, destinations and structures side-by-side before committing to any single provider — the way hotel buyers use Booking.com.

This is structurally good for the category. Side-by-side comparison forces operator transparency on the metrics that matter (resale data, fee structures, rotation rules). The operators best-positioned for the next decade are those that compete on those metrics rather than on marketing.

## Market size in one page

We deliberately avoid putting a single billion-dollar figure on the category — credible sizing of co-ownership has historically been weak and we won't pretend otherwise. What we can say with confidence:

- **Across the established single-property operators globally**, total live listings number in the low thousands (directionally — most operators publish their inventory, and counts can be re-verified independently). The largest single operator currently lists in the high hundreds. The next tier of operators list between 30 and 120 properties each.
- **The European single-property co-ownership inventory has roughly tripled since 2022.** Pre-2022 the category was effectively a handful of pilot projects; in 2026 it is a working category with established price discovery, resale precedent and operator competition.
- **Demand directional signals from independent marketplace data** show year-on-year enquiry growth in the high double-digit percentages through 2025 and into 2026.

We expect 2026-2028 to see the inventory roughly double again, with the growth concentrated in Mediterranean Europe and to a lesser extent the European Alps.

## Methodology note (page 4)

This report is built on three data sources:

1. **Operator-published inventory** — every single-property operator covered publishes its listings on its own site. We capture inventory snapshots quarterly and verify against the operator's stated count.
2. **Regulatory filings where available** — UK Companies House, Danish CVR, and equivalent registries provide independent verification of corporate facts (founding year, registered address, audited turnover for the small handful of operators that file accounts).
3. **Independent marketplace data from Co-Ownership Property** — directional demand patterns from COP's own enquiry data, treated as **directional only** throughout. No specific enquiry counts, no specific listings, no buyer identities. The methodology section in the appendix details the anonymisation approach.

Where a claim cannot be independently verified from at least one of these three sources, the claim is flagged as estimated or omitted entirely. Every number in this report has a footnote.

## What this report does not do

Three things we deliberately don't attempt:

1. **We don't rank operators.** Each operator serves a distinct geography, buyer profile and price point. A single-figure "best operator" ranking would mislead more than it informs.
2. **We don't publish per-operator annual fee comparisons.** The data needed to compare like-for-like is not yet publicly reliable across operators. We instead publish category-wide fee bands.
3. **We don't forecast individual operator outcomes.** We forecast the category. Operator-specific calls are beyond what the data supports.
