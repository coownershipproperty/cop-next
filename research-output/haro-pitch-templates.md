## How HARO / Qwoted / Featured.com work

Each platform sends daily emails listing journalist queries: *"Looking for a real estate expert who can comment on the rise of fractional ownership in Europe"*, etc. Whoever responds first and best gets quoted, with a backlink to their site.

**The job:** monitor 3 emails per day, respond to relevant ones within 90 minutes, get quoted in 2-4 articles per month over 12 months.

**The trick:** journalists are time-poor. They pick the answer that is (1) on-topic, (2) quotable, (3) attributable to someone with credible credentials, and (4) submitted fast. Most experts overthink the response and lose to faster, more concise replies.

### Template 2 — "What's the difference between fractional ownership and a timeshare?"

**Pitch:**

> Hi [journalist], this is my single most-asked question — happy to put it on the record.
>
> **The cleanest one-line:** "In a timeshare, you own time. In fractional ownership, you own a slice of real estate. The first depreciates; the second tracks the underlying property value."
>
> **The structural quote:** "The legal hinge is the LLC. A fractional share is a documented ownership stake in a property-holding company; your name sits on the LLC's membership register. A timeshare is a contractual right to use a property — no deed, no equity, no asset on your balance sheet."
>
> **The resale quote:** "Look at the resale data. Fractional shares in established markets typically clear within three to six months at or near entry price. Timeshare resale markets routinely transact at a fraction of original purchase."

### Template 6 — "What should buyers ask before purchasing a fractional share?"

**Pitch:**

> Hi [journalist], the four questions that separate a good operator from a fragile one:
>
> 1. **"What is the average resale time on shares sold through your platform in the past 24 months?"** — operators who can answer with a specific number have a functioning secondary market; those who deflect don't.
> 2. **"What percentage of shares listed for resale in the past 12 months have closed?"** — same point, harder number to fudge.
> 3. **"How does the rotation system allocate peak weeks?"** — this single answer determines whether the ownership experience is good or bad over a decade.
> 4. **"What happens if your company stops trading?"** — the underlying property is in a property-specific LLC, but the management transition matters.
>
> **Quote:** "A credible operator answers all four directly. An operator that deflects on resale data in particular is a meaningful warning sign — it usually means the secondary market is thinner than the marketing suggests."
>
> [credentials block]

## Things to NOT do

- Don't pitch every query — relevance matters more than volume
- Don't use the same quote across multiple platforms in the same week — journalists notice
- Don't break embargo if a query specifies one
- Don't ask the journalist for the backlink upfront — it's understood; pestering kills the relationship
- Don't comment on individual operators in a way that could be litigated as commercial disparagement (e.g. don't say "Operator X's resale times are terrible") — speak to the category dynamic instead
