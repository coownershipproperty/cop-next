## Why this report is the single most valuable thing COP builds in 2026

Knight Frank's Prime Global Cities Index gets cited thousands of times per year. They didn't earn those backlinks by writing thousands of guest posts — they earned them by *being the source*. There's literally no comparable index for fractional/co-ownership. COP is uniquely positioned to own that position because:

1. We aggregate hundreds of listings across the most established single-property co-ownership operators (only company doing this in our niche)
2. We've done deep research on 17 operators worldwide (the operators dataset)
3. We have CRM data on demand patterns
4. We have direct relationships with the major operators we work with

**One well-distributed report = compounding citation value for years.**

> **Editorial focus:** the report focuses on the *single-property 1/8-share model* (the COP-style category — buyer owns 1/8 of one specific home). Portfolio-fractional operators (e.g. members buy access to a rotating pool of homes) are covered in a single short sub-section as "an adjacent model" — interesting context, not the headline.

> **Design rule:** the finished PDF must NOT look AI-generated. Press will notice and discount it. The designer brief is "Knight Frank-quality, hand-set, slightly imperfect" — real photography (not stock), bespoke charts (not auto-generated dashboards), proper typography, consistent margin work.

## Report structure (10 sections, ~80 pages)

### Section 1 — Executive summary (4 pages)
- 5 headline findings (the ones we want cited)
- 1-page market-size estimate
- 1-page methodology note (transparency builds trust)

### Section 2 — The state of the category in 2026 (8 pages)
- Total inventory across operators (directional; sourced from operator websites + public data)
- Year-over-year growth signals (use CRM directional data: "demand for fractional ownership grew materially in 2025-2026")
- Geographic concentration — Mediterranean Europe leading
- Operator-level footprints (Pacaso strongest in US; MYNE the broadest European footprint)
- The category split: **focus on the single-property 1/8-share model**; portfolio/membership-pool models flagged briefly as an adjacent category

### Section 3 — Demand patterns (10 pages) — uses COP CRM data
- Top 10 destinations by enquirer demand (per CRM, directional)
  - Côte d'Azur leading, followed by Costa del Sol, Mallorca, French Alps
- Budget distribution
  - Most enquiries fall in the €100-200k and €200-400k bands
  - <€100k is smallest, €700k+ is also relatively small
- Lead source patterns (what marketing channels work for fractional)
- Seasonal patterns (when do people enquire about fractional?)
- ⚠️ Avoid: specific number claims, specific listing names, specific buyer nationalities (the CRM destination chart doesn't show nationality)

### Section 4 — Pricing benchmarks (8 pages)
- Average share price by destination (from aggregated COP listings + operator data)
- Cost-per-night equivalents (what the ~45 days/year of usage actually cost) — directional bands, not per-operator
- Total cost of ownership directional models — fractional vs whole vs rental for €1M, €2M, €5M property tiers
- ⚠️ **NOT covered:** annual fees broken down by operator — we don't have reliable cross-operator data, so we won't pretend we do. Replace with "fees vary by operator; ranges typical of the category are €X-Y per share per year" (directional bands only).

### Section 5 — The operator landscape (12 pages) — uses operators dataset
- **Primary focus:** single-property 1/8-share operators (the COP-style model). Profiles of the most established names worldwide.
- **Short adjacent-model sub-section (2 pages, end of section):** "Portfolio / membership-pool operators" briefly explains the alternative model (members access a rotating pool of homes) and names the credible operators in that space as context, without endorsing it as the headline category.
- Geographic coverage matrix (single-property operators only)
- Pricing tier comparison (single-property operators only)
- Resale/exit mechanics (where data is public — flag clearly where it is not)
- Operator footprint by destination (illustrative, not ranked)

### Section 6 — Destination deep-dives (12 pages)
- 6 destination spotlights: Mallorca, Côte d'Azur, French Alps, Lake Como, Algarve, Aspen
- For each: how many operators serve it, avg share price, demand trend, what's available
- Pulled from COP aggregated data

### Section 7 — The exit/resale market (6 pages)
- What we actually know about resale times (Pacaso 99-day US avg, Vivla <4-week Spain avg, MYNE undisclosed, August 15+ documented resales)
- Why the secondary market matters for buyer confidence
- The honest answer: the resale market is still maturing across operators

### Section 8 — Legal & tax landscape (6 pages)
- LLC / SCI / SL structures across markets
- Cross-border ownership rules summary
- Recent regulatory changes (Spanish golden visa changes, etc.)
- Tax efficiency of LLC structure vs whole ownership

### Section 9 — Looking ahead: 2026-2030 forecasts (5 pages)
- 5 predictions for the category (focused on the single-property model)
- New single-property entrants we expect
- Regulatory horizon
- Brief mention of the portfolio-fractional trend as an adjacent market signal — not the headline forecast

### Section 10 — Methodology, sources, acknowledgements (4 pages)
- Full source list (operators, partner data, public regulatory filings, trade press)
- COP CRM data methodology (anonymised, directional)
- Author bio + credentials
- How to cite this report
- Suggested citation format

## Design — three options

| Option | Cost | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI-assisted design** (Canva + Claude prompts for diagrams) | $0 | Free, fast | ❌ Looks AI-generated; trade press will sniff it out instantly and discount the data |
| **Freelance designer** (Upwork, 99designs) | $1,500-3,000 | Custom, polished, hand-set look | Time + back-and-forth |
| **Knight Frank-style hire** (specialist real-estate research designer) | $5,000-10,000 | Stunning, immediately credible | Cost |

**Recommendation:** Option 2 (~$1,500-2,500 freelance). The designer brief is explicit: **the report must NOT look AI-generated**. That means:
- Real (licensed) photography only — no generative/stock-looking imagery
- Bespoke charts drawn by hand or in a vector tool — no auto-generated dashboards, no default chart-library styling
- Editorial typography (proper serif heads, real grid, generous margins)
- Slightly imperfect human touches — small caption variations, occasional pull quotes, sidebars
- A short author photo + biography spread, hand-signed foreword from David

Press editors look at PDFs all day and instantly discount anything that pattern-matches to AI output. The content credibility falls with the design credibility.
